How to work out how much carpet you need

Broadloom carpet is cut from a fixed-width roll, so you pay for full-width strips rather than bare floor square footage. Planning strip count and lay direction first prevents expensive seam surprises.

Step-by-step measuring walkthrough

  1. Measure the longest length and widest width of the room, measuring into doorways, alcoves, closets, and bay windows.
  2. Confirm the roll width stocked for your chosen carpet—commonly 12 ft (or 15 ft) in the US and 4 m (or 5 m) in the UK and Europe.
  3. Add a trimming allowance of about 3 to 4 in (8 to 10 cm) at each end of every strip for stretching and tucking against baseboards.
  4. Check whether changing the lay direction reduces strip count or linear length, keeping in mind that all pieces must keep pile running the same way.

What to enter in the calculator

  • Room length: Longest measurement including doorways and closets.
  • Room width: Widest perpendicular measurement.
  • Carpet roll width: Common US rolls: 12 ft or 15 ft; UK/EU rolls: 4 m or 5 m.
  • Trim at each end of strip: Typical stretching/tucking allowance: 3 to 4 in (0.25–0.33 ft / 0.10 m).
  • Compare changing the common lay direction: Checks whether running all strips the opposite way uses less linear roll.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Multiplying room length × width and ordering that square footage. A 14 × 14 ft room needs two 12-ft-wide strips unless a 15-ft roll is used.
  • Turning an off-cut 90 degrees to fill a side strip. Carpet pile reflects light differently across angles and looks like a mismatched color patch.
  • Placing seams right across doorways or main traffic paths where foot traffic frays joins fastest.
  • Forgetting stairs, landings, or pattern repeats on striped and plaid carpets.

Practical ordering checklist

  • Ask the installer whether a 15 ft (or 5 m) roll eliminates a seam completely—it can cost less overall than wasting half of a narrower roll.
  • Keep carpet pile direction consistent from room to room across adjoining doorways.
  • Request a professional cutting diagram for irregular L-shaped rooms, stairs, or patterned carpets.
