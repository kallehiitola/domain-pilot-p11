How to work out how much carpet you need

Estimate full-width strips and linear roll length for a rectangular room. This distinguishes the area you cover from the carpet area you buy.

Quick answer: A 5 × 4 m room, a 4 m roll and 0.10 m trimming at each end need 5.20 linear metres, or 20.80 m² of carpet.

Step by step

  1. Measure the longest length and widest width of the room, including door openings and bay windows.
  2. Check which roll widths the shop sells. Carpet is cut from a roll of fixed width, so you buy a length of the full roll width.
  3. Add a trim allowance at each end of every strip for fitting against the walls.
  4. Compare both laying directions. One direction often needs less roll length or fewer seams.

What to enter in the calculator

  • Room length
  • Room width
  • Carpet roll width
  • Trim at each end of a strip
  • Allow changing the common lay direction: choose the option that matches your layout or product instructions.

How the calculation works

Divide room width by roll width and round up to whole strips. Add trimming allowance at both ends of each strip, then multiply by strip count. If changing the common lay direction is allowed, compare both room orientations and use the smaller linear length.

Common mistakes

  • Ordering by floor area. You pay for the full roll width of every strip, including the off-cut.
  • Placing seams across doorways or main walkways, where they wear fastest.
  • Laying strips with the pile running in different directions, which makes them look like different shades.

Another worked example

A 5 × 5 m room from a 4 m roll needs two 5 m strips: 10 linear metres, despite a floor area of 25 m².

What changes the estimate

All strips use one common direction. Leave rotation off when pile direction, matching or the installation plan fixes the lay. Stairs, irregular rooms, doorways, pattern repeats, joins and reusing offcuts require an installer’s cutting plan. Supplier selling increments are not included.
